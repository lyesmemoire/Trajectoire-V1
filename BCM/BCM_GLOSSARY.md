## Glossary

### A

**Adaptation**: The process of modifying behavior to improve performance in changing environments.

**Adaptation Theory**: BCM-013, the universal theory of adaptation for cognitive systems.

**Algorithm**: A step-by-step procedure for solving a problem or achieving a goal.

**Algorithm Catalog**: BCM-ALGORITHM-CATALOG, the comprehensive catalog of cognitive algorithms.

**Attention**: The cognitive process of selectively concentrating on a discrete aspect of information while ignoring other perceivable information.

### B

**Belief**: A mental state that represents an attitude towards a proposition.

**Belief Theory**: BCM-006, the universal theory of belief for cognitive systems.

**Blueprint Cognitive Model (BCM)**: The universal computational theory of cognition for Blueprint Enterprise.

**Blueprint DSL**: The domain-specific language for defining cognitive systems.

### C

**Cognitive Graph**: A graph representation of cognitive structures with nodes and edges.

**Cognitive Graph Model**: BCM-016, the universal cognitive graph model.

**Cognitive Guarantees**: Formal guarantees for cognitive system behavior.

**Cognitive Guarantees**: BCM-020, the universal cognitive guarantees document.

**Cognitive Mathematics**: The mathematical foundations for cognitive systems.

**Cognitive Mathematics**: BCM-019, the universal cognitive mathematics document.

**Cognitive Metrics**: Measurements of cognitive system performance and behavior.

**Cognitive Metrics**: BCM-018, the universal cognitive metrics document.

**Cognitive State Machine**: A state machine representing cognitive states and transitions.

**Cognitive State Machine**: BCM-017, the universal cognitive state machine.

**Confidence**: A multi-dimensional vector representing the degree of certainty in cognitive operations.

**Confidence Theory**: BCM-004, the universal theory of confidence.

**Consolidation**: The process of strengthening memory over time.

**Context**: The set of circumstances or facts surrounding a cognitive operation.

### D

**Decision**: The selection of an action from a set of alternatives.

**Decision Theory**: BCM-009, the universal theory of decision for cognitive systems.

**Decay**: The gradual loss of memory strength over time.

**Determinism**: The property of cognitive operations producing the same output given the same input.

### E

**Evidence**: Information that supports or contradicts a belief or hypothesis.

**Evidence Theory**: BCM-003, the universal theory of evidence for cognitive systems.

**Eviction**: The removal of memory when capacity is exceeded.

**Event**: A discrete occurrence that triggers cognitive operations.

**Event Registry**: BCM-EVENT-REGISTRY, the comprehensive registry of cognitive events.

**Execution**: The process of carrying out a plan or decision.

### F

**Feedback**: Information about the results of cognitive operations used for learning and adaptation.

**Forbidden Behavior**: A behavior that cognitive systems must not perform.

**Formal Specification**: BCM-FORMAL-SPECIFICATION, the formal specification of the BCM.

### G

**Generalization**: The process of applying learned knowledge to new situations.

**Goal**: A desired state or outcome that guides cognitive operations.

**Graph**: A structure consisting of nodes and edges representing relationships.

**Graph Registry**: BCM-GRAPH-REGISTRY, the comprehensive registry of cognitive graphs.

**Guarantee**: A formal promise about cognitive system behavior.

### H

**Hypothesis**: A tentative explanation or prediction that can be tested and validated.

**Hypothesis Theory**: BCM-007, the universal theory of hypothesis for cognitive systems.

**Heuristic**: A rule-of-thumb strategy for solving problems efficiently.

### I

**Inference**: The process of deriving conclusions from premises.

**Invariant**: A property that always holds true for a cognitive system.

**Knowledge**: Organized information about the world.

**Knowledge Theory**: BCM-005, the universal theory of knowledge for cognitive systems.

### L

**Learning**: The process of acquiring knowledge and improving performance through experience.

**Learning Theory**: BCM-012, the universal theory of learning for cognitive systems.

**Limitation**: A constraint or restriction on cognitive capabilities.

### M

**Mathematical Foundations**: BCM-MATHEMATICAL-FOUNDATIONS, the mathematical foundations of the BCM.

**Memory**: The storage and retrieval of information for future use.

**Memory Theory**: BCM-011, the universal theory of memory for cognitive systems.

**Meta-Reasoning**: The process of reasoning about one's own reasoning processes.

**Meta-Reasoning Theory**: BCM-014, the universal theory of meta-reasoning.

**Metric**: A standard of measurement for cognitive operations.

### N

**Node**: An element in a graph representing a cognitive entity.

**Observation**: The process of acquiring information from the environment.

**Observation Theory**: BCM-001, the universal theory of observation.

### O

**Object**: A cognitive entity with properties and behaviors.

**Object Registry**: BCM-OBJECT-REGISTRY, the comprehensive registry of cognitive objects.

**Ontology**: A formal representation of knowledge as a set of concepts and relationships.

### P

**Perception**: The process of interpreting sensory information.

**Perception Theory**: BCM-002, the universal theory of perception.

**Planning**: The process of creating a sequence of actions to achieve a goal.

**Planning Theory**: BCM-010, the universal theory of planning.

**Performance**: The effectiveness of cognitive operations.

**Proof**: A logical argument demonstrating the truth of a theorem.

### R

**Reasoning**: The process of drawing conclusions from premises through logical inference.

**Reasoning Theory**: BCM-008, the universal theory of reasoning.

**Relation**: A connection or association between cognitive entities.

**Relation Registry**: BCM-RELATION-REGISTRY, the comprehensive registry of cognitive relations.

**Retrieval**: The process of accessing stored information.

### S

**Self Evaluation**: The process of assessing one's own performance and capabilities.

**Self Evaluation Theory**: BCM-015, the universal theory of self evaluation.

**State**: A condition or mode of being of a cognitive system.

**State Registry**: BCM-STATE-REGISTRY, the comprehensive registry of cognitive states.

**Storage**: The process of preserving information for future use.

### T

**Theorem**: A statement that has been proven based on axioms.

**Traceability**: The ability to trace cognitive operations to their source.

**Transfer**: The application of learning from one domain to another.

**Transition**: A change from one state to another.

### U

**Universality**: The property of being applicable across all cognitive systems.

### V

**Verification**: The process of confirming the correctness of cognitive operations.

**Violation**: A breach of a cognitive guarantee.

### W

**Working Memory**: Short-term memory for current cognitive operations.
